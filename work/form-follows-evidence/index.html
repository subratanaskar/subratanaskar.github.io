The call

At 9:40 pm, a homeowner in Salt Lake returns to find her flat broken into. She calls 112 and walks to the local police station. An FIR is registered and the offence sections — the U/S — are fixed.

Securing the scene

The Investigating Officer cordons the flat. A second officer records the scene on a phone — under BNSS §176(3), offences punishable with 7+ years need a forensic expert and video of the collection.

Lifting the evidence

A forensic officer lifts a broken latch, a glove print and shoe-mark casts. Every item gets a number and a bag, on the spot.

Sealed in wax

Back at the station, each exhibit is wrapped, tied and closed with a wax seal. The seal impression becomes the proof that nobody opened it.

The form is written

Late that night, the Sub-Inspector fills the Case Exhibit Submission Form: case history, every exhibit, and what the lab must examine. This sheet is now the only voice of the case.

Authorised to send

Next morning, the Superintendent signs and stamps the Authorization Certificate, forwarding the exhibits to CFSL Kolkata.

At the lab counter

A messenger carries the parcels and the form to the lab. The receipt clerk checks every seal against the form before accepting anything.

Reading the request

A scientist reads the examination request to decide which tests to run. Her report goes back to the IO — and, months later, to the court.

Eight moments from a crime to a forensic report, told as one case. The Case Exhibit Submission Form is unseen at the scene, but every decision made there appears on it. Gold tags highlight when the form is written, signed, checked, or read.

A forensic redesign of India's Case Exhibit Submission Form , the critical document that travels with physical evidence from crime scene to laboratory.

Directorate of Forensic Science Services

Introduction

A Case Exhibit Submission Form is an official document used to securely transfer physical evidence or documents from law enforcement to a forensic laboratory (e.g., CFSL) for analysis. It lists evidence, details the case, and ensures chain of custody. Synonyms include Forwarding Note, Evidence Submission Form, or Chain of Custody Form.

Brief

Select a physical government form to audit and redesign, making the form filling process easier for the user with easy to understand information.

Objective

This project aims to redesign this form with survivors at the centre, drawing from global best practices, psychological safety, and visual clarity.

Who uses this form?

Investigating Officers (IOs) at police stations across India fill out this form before dispatching any physical evidence — fingerprints, biological samples, documents, weapons, narcotics — to the CFSL for forensic analysis.

What's at stake?

An incorrectly or incompletely filled form can delay analysis, compromise chain of custody, or — in worst cases — render forensic evidence inadmissible in court. The form is both a logistical and legal document.

The hidden complexity

The IO must detail the case history, list exhibits, explain how each was found, specify needed analyses, and get a senior officer's signature—all on a single 3-page form.

Physical, not digital

This form accompanies sealed evidence parcels, printed and filled by hand or typewriter, signed, wax-sealed, and delivered by mail or hand, so clear layout and legibility are essential.

0 / 7

Nobody has a priority field

No compared form lets an officer flag urgency. Adding Routine / Expedited lets India move ahead instead of catching up.

India leads

The forwarding mechanism is unique

The Authorization Certificate gives a legal sign-off most foreign forms lack. The redesign keeps it and repeats FIR No., P.S. and U/S inside it.

US labs

The form is a conversation

Alaska’s crime lab calls its request form a “communication device” — the redesign treats every field as a question the scientist would otherwise phone to ask.

UK

Standards are statutory

England & Wales’ Forensic Science Regulator Code became legally binding on 2 Oct 2023. India has no such regulator, so the form itself becomes the de-facto standard.

NIJ

Narrative matters

US guidance recommends sending a case narrative or police report with evidence. The redesign’s guided “Description of the Case” does exactly this.

Who must sign: The Forwarding letter and the Authorization Certificate (Page 6) must be signed and stamped by a judicial Officer, SP/DCP or above, Senior Officer equivalent to Gr. ‘A’ Gazetted Officer of Mid/Senior Level Management in Autonomous Bodies, Public Sector Undertakings and Nationalized Banks etc.

Separate Parcels: The Forwarding letter and duly filled in “Case Exhibit Submission Form” and Case Exhibit(s) in sealed parcel(s) must be sealed and forwarded in separate parcels. A copy of FIR, Medico - legal Examination Report, Post-Mortem Report must be attached with the form where applicable.

3

Packaging Standards: All exhibits must be properly labelled (Exhibit Number, Case Number, P.S name), sealed and initiated by the Investing Officer. Biological evidence must be air-dried before packaging in bags. Digital devices must have a Consent or Search Warrant copy attached.

B. Class Cases: Higher/Lower Courts, Autonomous Bodies, PSUs, National Banks: Rs. 5,000.

Lab Hours: The CFSL, Kolkata functions from 9.30 AM to 6.00 PM on all working days and remains closed on Saturday, Sunday and other public holidays.

Messenger ID: Messenger(s) visiting the laboratory for official work of case exhibit submission or collection of forensic examination report should possess valid Authorization and official ID Card as proof.

India’s forensic science workload, in context

New criminal laws, more registered crime and a national push for forensic infrastructure all mean more forms, more exhibits and less tolerance for incomplete paperwork.

More cases → more forms

Every extra forensic case starts with this sheet. A form that takes 10 minutes less, saves thousands of officer-hours a year.

BNSS → richer scenes

Mandatory scene visits and video create photo and video references the old form had no place for. The redesign adds them in Crime Scene Overview.

More labs → one standard

With new CFSLs opening, a single structured form keeps submissions comparable across labs and divisions.

At first glance, the form appears functional. But examined through the lens of the user, a police officer filling this in at a police station, under time pressure, often by hand, significant friction points emerge on every page.

The actual flow in Crime Scene and Case Inspection is not same as the current flow of the form as mentioned by the Sub Inspector Shashank Vishwakarma.

After getting the all information from the existing form, i have just sorted down the whole information needed for the redesign of the form

UI vs UX problem

These two types of problems need different solutions. Confusing them is itself a design mistake.

Page by page analysis of the current form is required to better understand issues

UI Impact

Officer misreads the form

Fields blur together. The officer writes in the wrong area.

→

UX Impact

Officer fills incorrect info

Guidance is absent. The officer guesses what to write.

→

Compound Effect

Lab receives incomplete form

→

Both problems together create systematic submission failures.

Worst Case

Analysis delayed or rejected

Evidence waits. Justice is delayed. Cases go delayed.
